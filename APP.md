# App Guide — What the Bill Payment Tracker Does

This page explains, in plain language, everything you'll see and can do in the Bill Payment Tracker. You don't need any technical knowledge to use the app or to understand this guide.

## The big picture

You tell the app three things:
1. **How much money you have right now** (your starting balance).
2. **How much money you expect to receive** before your bills are due (like a paycheck).
3. **What bills you need to pay** (rent, credit cards, insurance, and so on).

The app then does all the math for you — showing you how much you'll have left after every bill is paid, warning you if you're about to run low on money, and helping you keep everything organized.

## The top of the page

**Title and date note** — At the top you'll see "Bill Payment Tracker" and a small note under it telling you the app's default due date for this pay period (either the 15th of the month, or the last few days of the month, depending on where you are right now).

**Sun/moon button** (top right) — Click this to switch between a dark background (easier on the eyes at night) and a light background (easier to read in bright rooms). Your choice is remembered automatically.

## The four number boxes

Right under the title you'll see four boxes at a glance:
- **Monthly bills** — the total of everything you owe this month.
- **Paid this month** — how much of that you've already paid.
- **Remaining** — how much is still left to pay.
- **Progress** — what percentage of your bills you've paid so far.

## Your balance and income

Two boxes let you type in:
- **Starting balance** — how much money is in your account right now.
- **Expected income** — money you know is coming in soon, like a paycheck.

Whatever you type here updates everything else on the page instantly.

## The big balance banner

This large, colorful box shows your **current balance** — your starting balance, plus expected income, minus everything you've already paid. Its color tells you how things are going:
- 🟦 **Blue/teal** — You're in good shape.
- 🟨 **Amber/yellow** — You're getting close to your safety limit. Be careful.
- 🟥 **Red (and gently pulsing)** — You've dropped below your safety limit. Stop paying bills for now.

The app always keeps a safety cushion of **$900** — it will warn you before letting you dip below that.

## Payment progress bar

A simple progress bar showing what share of your bills are paid, plus a count like "3 / 11" (three bills paid out of eleven total).

## Savings transfer suggestion

If you've paid every bill and still have extra money above your $900 safety cushion, this box turns green and tells you exactly how much you could safely move into savings. If you still have unpaid bills, it reminds you to finish paying those first.

## Spending by card & Monthly trend

Two small charts:
- **Spending by card** — a simple bar for each bill, so you can see at a glance which ones cost the most.
- **Monthly trend** — after you've used the app for a couple of months, this shows a bar for each past month, so you can compare how much you owed and how much you paid over time. Until you've completed at least one month, it just shows a friendly note that it will fill in over time.

## Switching between List view and Calendar view

Two buttons let you choose how to see your bills:
- **List** — every bill shown as its own card, one under another.
- **Calendar** — a monthly calendar grid, with small colored tags on the day each bill is due (green if paid, red if overdue, blue if it's still coming up). Arrows let you flip to the previous or next month.

Next to the view buttons, a **search box** lets you type a bill's name to find it quickly, and a **dropdown filter** lets you narrow the list down to only unpaid, only paid, only overdue, or only bills due soon.

## Each bill card (List view)

Every bill shows:
- A **checkbox** — tick it to mark the bill as paid.
- The **bill's name** — click it to rename it.
- A **category tag** (like "Housing" or "Credit Card").
- A **due-status tag** telling you how urgent it is — red for overdue or due today, orange for due very soon, amber for due within a week, and a plain gray tag if it's not due for a while yet.
- A 🔁 symbol if the bill repeats every month.
- A red **Delete** button.

Underneath, you can edit:
- **Amount** — you can even type simple math here, like `100+50`, and it will add it up for you.
- **Confirmation #** — a spot to save a confirmation number after you pay.
- **Due date** — pick a date from the calendar.
- **Category** — choose what kind of bill it is.
- **Recurring** — a button to mark whether this bill repeats every month.
- **Balance after** — shows what your account balance would be, right after this particular bill, in the order your bills are listed. This number is color-coded the same way as the big balance banner.

Bills you've already paid look slightly faded, so your eye is naturally drawn to what's still left to do.

## The safety warning pop-up

If checking off a bill as paid would drop your balance below your $900 safety cushion, the app **won't let it happen automatically**. Instead, a red warning pop-up appears telling you exactly what your balance would become, and the bill stays unpaid until you decide what to do.

## Summary box

A short, simple recap near the bottom: total bills, how much you've paid, how much is unpaid, your expected income, and your final balance (shown in red if it's below your safety cushion).

## The action buttons

- **+ Add bill** — opens a small form to add a new bill (name, amount, category, due date, and whether it repeats monthly).
- **↻ Start new month** — use this once you've finished paying everything for the month. It:
  - Saves this month's totals so they show up in the "Monthly trend" chart later.
  - Rolls any repeating bills forward to next month's due date, and clears their "paid" checkmark so you can pay them again.
  - Removes one-time bills you've already paid (since they're done).
  - Leaves any unpaid one-time bills untouched, so you don't lose track of something you still owe.
- **Import backup** — lets you load a previously saved backup file.
- **Export CSV** — saves your bill list as a spreadsheet-friendly file you can open in Excel or Google Sheets.
- **Download backup** — saves everything (your bills, balances, and history) as a file on your computer, in case you want a copy or need to move to a new computer.

## Notifications

Whenever you do something — add a bill, pay a bill, import a backup — a small message briefly pops up in the bottom-right corner of the screen to confirm it happened, then disappears on its own after a few seconds.

## Your data is safe and private

Everything you type is saved automatically, right inside your own web browser, on your own computer. Nothing is sent over the internet, and no one else can see it. If you ever switch computers or browsers, use the **Download backup** button first, then **Import backup** on the new one to bring everything with you.
